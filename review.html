Changes

The improvements, screen by screen

web_asset
Header (every page)

The SMCINEMA wordmark kept exactly as in your Figma (bold SM, light CINEMA), “Movies” added as the first nav item, equal spacing, a yellow active bar instead of the faint cyan underline, and search, notifications and your avatar grouped on the right exactly as in your Figma (search expands into a field when clicked).

Was: flat bar with a typed “SMCINEMA”, three nav items floating left of centre, icon-only search, generic user icon.

palette
One design system

One blue (#164ACF) for everything transactional, yellow only for loyalty and attention, one neutral ramp, two typefaces (Plus Jakarta Sans for headings and buttons, Inter for body and numbers), one button system, one chip, one rating badge, one date chip, one showtime button and one card style. Dark mode is a token swap, so it works on every page.

Was: five different blues, three typefaces that changed from page to page, four button radii, blurry raster MTRCB badges.

movie
Home

The print-ad carousel became a native hero: the banner is a dimmed backdrop under site typography, with an eyebrow, title, meta chips, two calls to action, thumbnail slide controls and dots. A quick-book bar (movie → cinema → date → time) sits on the hero edge. The grid has format filters, hover “Get tickets” overlays, Coming soon placeholders, an Experience teaser, promos and a footer.

Was: side-peek carousel cropped through faces, nine identical “Buy tickets” rectangles, no footer, no filters, no path to book from the top of the page.

local_activity
Movie details

Blurred-poster backdrop band, readable chips, a proper gallery (trailer tile with play button, photos, “View all” scrim), facts list, and a booking rail with the real price per format (“2D · Cinema 4 · ₱350 per seat”), a compact 7-day strip and a selection summary. Cinema change and cinema info are modals, so you never leave the page.

Was: “₱[price]” placeholder, “View all 12 photos” as blue text on a photo, date strip that scrolled off the card.

event_seat
Seats

Seat tiles with hatched “taken” seats (not colour alone), a visible screen band, wheelchair spaces, a quantity stepper that limits picks, “Pick best seats” that finds a middle block, removable seat chips, senior/PWD discounts that recalculate the total, and the change-showtime modal with the clear-seats warning from your prototype.

Was: black box where the poster should be, legend swatch that looked like a checkbox, hairline screen arc.

payments
Payment

Full-width section headings, a hold timer that really counts down with a shrinking bar and expiry dialog, inline validation for email and the 09XX mobile format, consistent logo tiles for GCash, Maya and card, card fields that appear when needed, and the missing “Use a reward” row so the voucher flow from Profile finally connects to checkout. Terms must be ticked before paying.

Was: headings wrapping in a narrow column, a static orange line that looked like progress, three unrelated chip colours, no way to use a reward.

qr_code_2
Confirmation, e-ticket, receipts

The booking-confirmed toast and the e-ticket modal open together after payment, exactly as in your flow. Tickets are “stubs” with a perforated edge and barcode; the e-ticket has a brand band, a large QR with a quiet zone, the reference in tabular figures and a receipt accordion. Past bookings open a receipt with VAT, payment method and invoice number.

location_on
Cinemas

Search that filters as you type, the location permission dialog with both outcomes (nearest-first with distances, or the “Location is off” notice), experience chips that explain why a branch is dimmed (“No IMAX”), a schedule panel with date strip, per-format prices and showtimes that jump straight to seats, a sold-out notice, and a stylised map in the cinema info modal with “Save as my cinema”.

star
Experience

Cards share one navy base with a single coloured glow per format so they read as one family; each CTA pre-filters the Cinemas page; the Event Screen banner opens a working inquiry form; the comparison table uses the check/dash convention consistently.

person
Profile

Your two profile variants merged: the “Your next movie” card and payment methods from the dashboard version, plus the points hero, settings and rewards from the main version. Every overlay works: edit profile (with the “Enter an 11-digit number…” validation), change password (live checklist), redeem (points math), reward voucher with QR, sign out, and a dark-mode toggle that changes the whole site.

smartphone
Responsive and accessible

Below 640px the header slims down (the avatar is hidden because the bottom tab bar already has Profile, and the notification panel opens as a full-width sheet instead of spilling off the screen), the bottom tab bar from your mobile frames appears; rails stack under content, the booking card moves above the synopsis, the seat map scrolls sideways, modals become bottom sheets. Every control is keyboard reachable with a visible focus ring, labels have proper contrast (no 11px text), and motion respects “reduce motion”.

Your call

Decisions I’d like your feedback on

1
Header style

I kept a solid SM-blue bar (refined) rather than a dark or two-tier header, because your approved design and the professor both saw blue. Say the word if you want to see a darker “cinematic” header instead.

2
Hero images

The hero uses your three banner images dimmed under the site text. The Avengers banner has its own “Tickets now on sale” lettering baked in; if you can export textless key art from Figma, it will look even cleaner.

3
Coming soon titles

Your Figma had no Coming soon content, so I added November 2026 releases (Godzilla Minus Zero, Wildwood, Look Back, Hexed) as text placeholders. Replace, remove, or send posters.

4
Yellow only for rewards

Yellow now marks loyalty, promos and active states; “Download PDF” and the “See showtimes” buttons became blue so the most important action on each page is always blue. Tell me if you prefer the yellow buttons from your frames.

5
Extras I added

Quick-book bar, promos row, experience teaser, “Add to calendar”, card fields, promo codes, reset-demo link. Any of these can go if you want the site closer to the Figma scope.

After your feedback I’ll apply the corrections and finish the build: remaining interactions, polishing, cross-browser checks and a final pass on the mobile layout.
